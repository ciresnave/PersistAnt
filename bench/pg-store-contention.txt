Reading, against the three refutation tests in the design note
1. Scaling. persist per call: 11.0 ms (W=1), 7.0, 6.3, 6.1 ms (W=8). W=8/W=1 = 0.55. The design said "refuted if below ~0.6", so on its letter
   this test refutes the strict form of my prediction ("stays near its 1-worker value"). What the data show instead is a PLATEAU: W=4 to W=8 is
   6.3 to 6.1 ms (0.96) while direct keeps falling (1.11 to 0.56 ms). The W=1 to W=2 drop is explained by W=1 also containing idle-in-transaction
   client time (a second worker fills it); the sampler shows `idle in transaction/ClientRead` at W=1. A ceiling of about 6 ms per store, however many workers,
   is a serial section; it is not proof of which lock.
2. Wait events. At W=8 the persist side shows lock waits (Lock/tuple + Lock/transactionid = 8,443 of about 11,600 sampled non-idle backends in the six top entries, about 73
   percent); the direct side shows none. This is the share the design said would support the hypothesis (above 20 percent). It does not name the lock row: pg_stat_activity
   does not say which tuple.
3. Control put_only (one plain put, no batch, same record, same direct upsert): ratio 0.99 at W=8, no growth. A single-statement write by the layer scales like the direct one.
   So the cost is in the BATCH path, not in the layer's storage layout or per-statement overhead.

Conclusion (what is measured, and what is not)
Measured: the store-token cost at 8 workers comes from the batch path serialising on lock waits (about 6 ms per store at any W of 4 or more, lock waits only on the persist side, a plain put does not
show it). Consistent with the code comment in crates/persistant-kv/src/on_relational.rs `batch_once` (a single `persistant_kv_batch` row taken FOR UPDATE by every batch), but I did not
test that row by itself: a variant of the layer with the lock row removed was not run, so "that row is the cause" is the best-supported reading, not a measurement.
NOT measured: a layer change (per-key or striped locking; the comment says the lock exists because racing batches deadlocked on missing rows, so removing it needs a design); MySQL; a request mix;
store at W above 8; the quiet-box rerun (still blocked: load above).
