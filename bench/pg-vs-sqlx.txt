PersistAnt's PostgreSQL layer against direct sqlx (ROADMAP row 30)

Measured: 2026-11-09, main cddaabc (0.16.13) plus the example in this change.
Rerun:    PERSISTANT_TEST_DATABASE_URL=postgres://... cargo run -q --release -p persistant-sqlx --example pg_vs_sqlx
Where:    Windows 11 developer machine; PostgreSQL 16.15 in a Docker Desktop container
          (postgres:16@sha256:ca0bd484cb98bf4b24eb1010e73fb3fcbd6714d240fbc1a10eea5b7dbecb641d),
          reached over localhost, so every call includes the VM's network hop.
What:     the same statements on the same sqlx pool, once through PgBackend (execute/query/begin) and once
          through sqlx::query on PgBackend::pool(). 400 operations per run per path, 50 warm-up, ABBA order,
          the two runs of a path pooled (800 samples). Three separate invocations.

Reading it: across the three runs the p50 ratio lies between 0.969 and 1.053 with no workload
consistently on one side of 1.0, and the p95 ratio between 0.885 and 1.103. That spread is the
machine's run-to-run noise. **At this scale, where every call crosses a 1.7 to 5.8 ms round trip to a
container, the layer's own cost cannot be told from zero.** That is a bound on what was measured, not a
claim that the layer is free: a database on the same host over a socket, or a pool of many connections
under load, would make the layer's share larger and was not measured. One connection, one operation at
a time: concurrency was not measured.

Not measured here: the key-value adapter's extra keys per record (the index layer) against the SQL
auth-framework runs today. That still needs `kv.speed`-style numbers against its real statements.
