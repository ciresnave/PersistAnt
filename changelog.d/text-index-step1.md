A text index as a layer over any kv backend with atomic batches and snapshot scans (memory, SQLite, PostgreSQL, MySQL, MariaDB; `Unsupported` on a blob store), behind the new cargo feature `text-index` of `persistant-kv` (root crate feature `text-index`); **no new dependency**. It takes terms with frequencies, not text, and hands out integers so a consumer ranks with its own formula: `postings(index, term, after, limit)` (`doc_id`, tf, ascending, paged, one snapshot), `doc_len`, `stats` (documents, total length) and `docs` (all document ids). `put` replaces a document (stale postings removed, totals moved by the difference) and `remove_many` removes documents with all their postings, each in **one batch** with a `Require` on the corpus totals and the document's record; a lost race retries up to 16 times. `search` (Any / All, term-frequency order, ties by `doc_id`) is a convenience; there is no BM25 and no expiry in v1. The corpus totals are one record every writer requires, so writers serialise (stated in the module docs). Design: `docs/DESIGN-TEXT-INDEX.md`, accepted by the PM and SageAnt 2026-11-15.

Verified against a plain map held by the test after every operation of a 120-step scripted sequence of puts, replaces and removals (stats, every document's length, every term's postings paged by two, the document list, Any and All queries, and the exact set of keys, so a replace or removal leaves nothing stale), on memory and on a real SQLite file, with concurrent writers on both (different documents: totals exact; one document, eight texts: exactly one survives). Three mutations each fail a test: dropping the stale-posting cleanup, skipping the posting deletes of `remove_many`, dropping the `Require` on the totals.
